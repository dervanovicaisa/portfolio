Case 05 / Independent project

Connecting travel stories with a working storefront.

Moja Srma is a website I built independently. It brings together a country atlas, travel guides and journal stories, postcards and other creative products, and a way for visitors to place orders.

01 / Context

One site, two connected journeys.

Visitors can explore destinations through the atlas, read guides and journal entries, and discover postcard designs. The site also has a catalog, cart and order flow for products.

Behind the public pages, an admin area supports editing content and products and reviewing orders. Building both sides meant thinking about what visitors need to see and what the owner needs to manage.

02 / Contribution

What I built.

  • Discovery: a country atlas that connects destinations with guides, journal stories and postcards.
  • Storefront: product pages, a cart and order forms for postcards and other creative products.
  • Content tools: an admin area for editing published articles and products and viewing orders.
  • Application logic: server-side order checks, email notifications and handling for uncertain order responses.
03 / Challenge

An order needs a clear outcome.

A visitor should not be told an order succeeded before the application can verify that it was saved. A slow or interrupted response also should not encourage a second, duplicate order. I worked through those states in a separate checkout analysis and reflected them in the order flow.

The application uses a request identifier when saving an order, checks current catalog prices on the server and provides a way to check an order's status when the first response is uncertain. Payment for digital postcards is arranged after the order rather than charged immediately at checkout.

Analysis sample

Designing checkout for uncertainty and recovery.

My independent analysis explores unknown shipping costs, duplicate submissions, server failures, stale cart state and unsupported delivery. It records possible states and recovery paths rather than claiming that every scenario occurred in production.

04 / Perspective

What this project shows.

Moja Srma gave me ownership of a product from content discovery through ordering and administration. It also pushed me to define rules for cases that are easy to miss in a design: changed prices, repeated submissions and uncertain responses. That combination of product thinking and implementation is what I want to bring to Business Systems Analysis.
